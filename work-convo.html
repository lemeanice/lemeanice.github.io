Conversational Notification Admin

Managing notifications internally means a separate admin UI and integration for every channel. I'm the sole PM on a small AI-native squad rebuilding that as a single conversational interface, where each new notification type becomes a "skill" the assistant learns instead of another tool to build. This is an internal, in-progress project, so the details here are kept high level.

Background
One interface instead of one tool per channel

Setting up notifications internally today means working across a patchwork of admin screens and integrations, roughly one per notification channel. Every new notification type carries a real cost: more UI, more backend wiring, more surface area to maintain.

The bet behind this project is that a conversational interface collapses that cost. Instead of building a new screen for each channel, the assistant "learns" a new notification type as a skill. The marginal cost of supporting the next channel drops toward the cost of teaching the assistant how it works.

What I own
Defining the product as the only non-engineering voice
Scope & requirements
Deciding what "done" means
Sole PM defining scope, requirements, and written exit conditions for a from-scratch product, on a team with no dedicated designer or QA. Features ship in small slices, each with an agreed exit bar before work starts.
Safety ✦ Human / AI boundary
Guardrails for a tool that can trigger real sends
Owned the rules for where the assistant must not guess, where it has to show inferred vs. confirmed values, and a mandatory human-approval gate before anything is sent. The cost of a wrong send is real, so the boundary is explicit.
Trade-offs
Sequencing and architecture calls
Made and documented product trade-offs with engineering, including resequencing the roadmap to take on a harder notification queue earlier, and a two-environment split that balances build speed against the risk of sending from the wrong place.
Stakeholders
Framing decisions for leadership
Presented roadmap, risks, and open trade-offs to cross-functional leads through structured async decks, laying out options with explicit pros and cons rather than pre-baked recommendations.
How we work
An AI-native squad, and the process to keep it honest

The team runs lean and fast: features shipping daily, a single daily working session in place of standups and sprints, and very little heavyweight documentation. That speed is the point, but it creates its own risks, mostly around review capacity and losing the record of why decisions were made.

So I built light process to match. A dated decision register captures engineering calls and their rationale, replacing an ad hoc chat log that didn't scale past two people. I also spec'd an internal chat-ops assistant that adds state and search on top of our bug and improvement log, with a two-key close control (engineer fixes, PM verifies) to preserve a quality check even without a QA function.

Where it's at
Phase 1 in internal testing

I planned and ran the project's first live internal usability test with a regional PM. The session surfaced a critical bug and validated real demand for bulk task creation, which fed directly back into roadmap sequencing discussions with leadership.

It's still early. The work now is widening the exit bar from "works in test" to "works across the full environment chain," and designing for bulk, multi-item use up front rather than retrofitting it later. I'll add more here as the project matures.
